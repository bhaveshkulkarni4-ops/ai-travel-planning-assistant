Payment by cash and cards is safe and efficient in Singapore. Tipping is not 
a common practice as service charge has been factored into the Goods and 
Services Tax (GST). As a tourist in Singapore, you can claim a refund on 7% 
GST under the Tourist Tax Refund Scheme if you make any purchase more 
than S$100 at the participating shops. Here’s how to go about it:
1. Look out for these signs at the shops.
2.  When making purchases from retailers who are on the Electronic 
Tourist Refund Scheme (eTRS), you are required to show your 
passport in person to prove your eligibility under the TRS. 
Photocopies or images of passports are not acceptable. The retailer 
will then issue an eTRS transaction to you at the point of purchase 
with your passport details. 
3.  Apply for your tax refund at the eTRS self-help kiosks located at 
Singapore Changi Airport’s Departure Check-In Hall (before 
departure immigration) and Departure Transit Lounge (after 
departure immigration); and at Seletar Airport Immigration & 
Checkpoints Authority (ICA) duty office. 
4.  At the eTRS kiosk, scan your passport to retrieve all your purchase 
details. 
5.  If you are departing from Changi Airport, you can choose to have the 
refund credited directly into your credit card, Alipay account (for Chinese 
citizens only) or get a cash refund. If you are departing from Seletar 
Airport, you will be asked to choose your refund to be made by credit card, 
Alipay (for Chinese citizens only) or bank cheque. Once you are done, 
review the outcome of your refund request shown on the eTRS kiosk to 
check if you need to go to the Customs Inspection counter, where custom 
officers may ask to see your purchases for further verification. 
6.  Due to the handling fees, the refunded amount will be less than the 
original GST amount charged. The details of your transactions can 
be verified by registering and logging into the eTRS e-Services at 
https://touristrefund.sg or via the “eTRS Singapore” app downloadable 
from Google Play or Apple App Store.

Singapore's diverse, multi-ethnic community offers many Muslim-friendly facilities. 
This includes facilities ranging from Halal-certified or Muslim-owned eating 
establishments to prayer rooms in most* major attractions, hotels and shopping 
malls. For a full list of mosques in Singapore, visit www.muis.gov.sg/mosque
* Do check in with the individual establishments on the facilities available.
Muslim-friendly Destination 
•  Keep to the left on escalators, especially during peak hours.
•  When taking public transport, always give way to alighting passengers. 
Board buses from the front door and exit by the rear door. Remember to 
press the ‘stop’ button early.
•  Some items like pets and durians are not allowed on public transport.
•  Singaporeans love queueing. If you see a queue, check out what’s at the 
front of it—you might be in for a treat!
•  Singapore does not have a tipping culture, but if you’d like to express your 
appreciation for the service you have received, please feel free to do so.

Fort Canning Park
A popular outdoor concert venue tucked away in 
a tranquil corner of our bustling city centre, Fort 
Canning is where Sir Stamford Raffles built his 
bungalow. As an ancient fort that has changed 
hands between various local and colonial 
powers, you can find the remnants of 18th and 
19th century Gothic-style gateways, cupolas and 
Singapore’s first Christian cemetery here. 
Explore its Spice Garden, ASEAN Sculpture 
Garden and hidden underground bunker, then 
visit its heritage museum to see photos 
depicting what these grounds once looked like.
